# Playtest Episode: The Lost Ball

## Position in the larger game

This is deliberately written as an episode from the middle of the child's first week. The protagonist already knows the route, recognizes the other children, and has watched their chalk-circle game before. The unresolved question is whether the group will make room for him.

## Playable arc

1. Arrive at the edge of the playground.
2. Move close enough to observe without interrupting.
3. Learn the simple rule of the chalk-circle game.
4. Watch the ball escape into a quiet garden.
5. Follow the child-sized route around the low wall.
6. Retrieve the muddy ball.
7. Carry it back.
8. Receive a small invitation rather than a dramatic friendship declaration.
9. Join one turn.
10. Hear Mom call and follow the warm doorway home.

## Why this slice

It exercises the project's highest-risk ideas in a small map:

- child-height third-person navigation;
- ordinary architecture made monumental by scale;
- social observation rather than a dialogue quiz;
- a recoverable emotional arc;
- one object interaction with a clear narrative meaning;
- the Emotional Lens changing camera, fog, light, sound, and attention without exposing an emotion meter;
- a complete beginning, turning point, and ending suitable for public playtesting.

## What is intentionally absent

- character customization;
- full home routines;
- branching dialogue trees;
- combat, failure, scoring, collectibles, achievements, or a friendship bar;
- accounts, analytics, or cloud saves;
- production character models or voice acting.

## Prototype technology decision

This playtest is dependency-free WebGL2 rather than the planned production PlayCanvas/TypeScript stack. That is intentional: the playtest URL has no build dependency, no external CDN, and can validate the emotional and spatial design before production architecture hardens. Successful mechanics can then be ported into the PlayCanvas vertical slice.
